What we help with

Humane exclusion, step by step

01 / INSPECTION

Inspection & documentation

Review signs of wildlife activity, likely access points, and visible damage. Photos can show roofline and attic areas that are difficult for a homeowner to access.

02 / EXIT

One-way exclusion

For bats and squirrels, a one-way device can let animals leave while preventing re-entry. The right schedule and approach depend on the animal and conditions at the property.

03 / SEAL

Seal & address damage

After animals have left, entry points can be sealed to reduce the chance of re-entry. The affected areas and completed work are documented so you can see what was addressed.

04 / PREVENT

Practical prevention

Get straightforward guidance on common access points and conditions around the home that may invite repeat activity.

Good to know

Bat work needs careful timing.

Alabama Cooperative Extension advises that bat exclusion only occur outside the pupping season (April through August) so young bats are not separated from their mothers. The specific plan and timing should be confirmed for the property.
