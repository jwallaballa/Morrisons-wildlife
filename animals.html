Alabama’s night flyers

Bats

Alabama’s native bats are insect-eaters. Some roost in buildings, and small gaps around rooflines, vents, or other joints can give them access.

Clues to look for
  • Repeated evening activity near one opening
  • Droppings or staining near a roost or entry point
  • Sounds from an attic, roof space, or wall void

Why the entry point matters

A roost can leave droppings, odor, and staining. Droppings may also attract insects. Sealing an opening while bats are still inside can trap them in the structure, so exclusion needs a deliberate plan.

Seasonal timing is important

Alabama Cooperative Extension advises bat exclusion only outside the pupping season, which it describes as April through August. The purpose is to avoid leaving young bats in the roost when adults leave. Timing for a specific property should be confirmed before work is scheduled.

Please don’t handle a bat.

If a person or pet may have had direct contact with a bat, contact a medical professional or local public health office for prompt advice.

At home in the treetops

Squirrels

Squirrels climb trees and buildings with ease. An opening at a soffit, fascia, vent, or roof edge can become a route into an attic or other sheltered space.

Clues to look for
  • Scratching or scurrying sounds overhead
  • Nesting material or damage near the roofline
  • Regular squirrel activity around one opening

How a small opening can become a big headache

Squirrels may chew wood and wiring while opening a route or building a nest. Once inside, nesting material and animal waste can affect insulation and leave cleanup work. Food sources such as bird seed, pet food, or open garbage can make a yard more attractive.

How one-way exclusion helps

A one-way device lets squirrels exit the structure but blocks re-entry. Once the animals are out, sealing the opening helps prevent another visit. Photos of the damage and finished work make hard-to-reach attic and roof areas easier to review.

A few easy prevention checks
  • ✓ Keep branches trimmed back from roof edges
  • ✓ Store bird seed and pet food in sealed containers
  • ✓ Secure lids on outdoor garbage
  • ✓ Check vents and roofline screens from the ground
